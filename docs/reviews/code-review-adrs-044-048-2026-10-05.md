### T1 · BLOCKING · ADR-047 Decision 5: the CV figures are stale and contradict the verdict and Decision 4 of the same ADR
- **ADR text:** "9B-MTP-on long-prompt CV 26.4%, 14B on the newer runtime 23.7%, both still refused after the top-up".
- **Source:** those are the pooled figures before the exclusion (#1665 c3855). After the gate exclusion, the verdict (`qwen35-9b-int8-verdict-2026-10-04.md`, lines 36-38 and 162) says both cells sit at "14 percent visit-to-visit spread, just inside my 15 percent limit ... less firm". It also says the exclusion "turned those ratios into stated ones". #1665 c3868 gives the 9B cell's CV as 14.4%.

- **Effect:** a reader of Decision 5 concludes the ratios are refused. A reader of the table concludes they are stated.
- **Not stated anywhere in the ADR:** the post-hoc exclusion is exactly what moved 10 ratios from refused to stated. That fact appears only obliquely, in the Alternatives bullet.

### T2 · BLOCKING · ADR-045: a re-visit trigger the LA never set is presented as met
- **ADR text:** in Alternatives, "Accept the egress gap permanently ... the re-visit trigger was a mechanism that works under Bitdefender, and #1671 found one". Consequences repeats it: "its re-visit trigger has been met".
- **Source:** #775 c1653, the LA decision, lists exactly three triggers: "a BD-firewall-module change, the VM leg going live, or any fleet-code egress incident". "A mechanism that works under Bitdefender" is not among them.
- **Contradiction inside the ADR:** the ADR's own Context paragraph lists the three triggers correctly.

- **Why this is blocking:** it rationalises reopening an LA-accepted posture after the fact. The direction is benign (it tightens), but the record should say "new evidence, not one of the named triggers".
