## Context

On 2026-09-17 a feasibility study (#1549) recommended **GO** on a standalone runtime-action-governance product for Windows PCs, with year one as a specification plus one open reference implementation. The LA took three of its six decisions the same day: go, the name ("The Governator"), and a separate repository — founded at `C:/Users/mrbla/governator` with its own rulebook, four ADRs and a sixteen-ticket Vikunja board (project 14). `docs/DECISION_REGISTER.md` recorded that position, including an asymmetric inbound/outbound boundary between the two codebases and BlarAI as the product's **first target in shadow mode**.

On **2026-09-18** the LA cancelled the venture. His reason, in his words: *"if we can never truthfully say 'agents cannot get around this' then we don't have a product."*

That follows directly from the study's own central measured finding. Every enforcement seam a third party can reach on Windows is **cooperative**: the agent's own program runs the governor and honours its answer, a command-line flag skips it (`--safe-mode`), a crashed hook fails **open**, and a shell command arrives as one opaque string — a network fetch inside `python -c` passed as an allowed Bash call. The one non-cooperative seam measured, a per-process firewall rule, **failed silently**: accepted by cmdlet, netsh, registry and event log, never a kernel filter, 37/37 requests succeeded, because a third-party security product owned the firewall categories. The study's answer to all of this was to **disclose coverage honestly rather than claim inescapability**. The LA judged that insufficient as a proposition to put in front of other people.

**One fact was put to him before he decided, and he decided anyway:** no security product in the category can make that claim either. Microsoft's own agent runtime protection sits on the same cooperative vendor hooks this study probed; antivirus, firewalls and endpoint detection all sit on bypassable seams and sell on coverage and honesty. The objection rules out this product only if it also rules out the category. He has the fact; the decision stands and is his to make — it is a claim decision about what may be said to other people, not a technical judgement.

**A first attempt at recording the cancellation was written too broadly** and would have misled a later session by reading as though the engineering were cancelled too. It was corrected the same day, before anything was built on it. This ADR records the corrected scope, because the register is what a future session trusts.

## Decision

1. **The standalone venture is CANCELLED.** Not deferred, not paused. There is **no check-back date and no condition under which it resumes by default.** What is cancelled is the product, its separate repository, its separate board, and everything that existed only because the thing was to be sold or given to other people: licence, trademark, approaching Microsoft or a foundation, legal form and the multi-user consent posture, a separate implementation language, adapting a third-party agent, the competitive check-back, and shipping the claim register *with a product*.

2. **The CAPABILITIES are not cancelled.** They are BlarAI's own engineering and they continue. The study is the evidence base for that work, not a casualty of the decision. Already in BlarAI: the action vocabulary (`shared/schemas/car.py`, extended under #1545), coverage disclosure (`docs/governance/auditability-card.md`, #1544), and the signed local record with verdict receipts at both live doors (#1542). Filed as BlarAI tickets when they were measured **absent**, and all four shipped on 2026-09-20: verify-your-own-record (#1581, `46c44454`), permission profiles (#1582, `336c620e`), the card as a screen (#1583, `7a03379f`), and a trust-spine liveness canary (#1584, `45a4da60`).

3. **The asymmetric boundary recorded in the register on 2026-09-17 is MOOT** — not repealed, not relaxed, *moot*: it governed traffic between two parties and there is no second party. Nothing of it is load-bearing any more, and no future decision should be derived from it. The locks it leaned on are BlarAI's own and are unchanged: the single-importer network scan, the armed socket guard, the dependency-truth and supply-chain-hash locks.

4. **`docs/research/runtime-action-governance-product-feasibility-2026-09-17-appendices/` is NOT to be deleted or archived.** It holds **measured probes of this machine** that outlive the product decision entirely: Windows 11 enforcement seams, isolation surfaces, and the TPM signing timings in appendix A4 seam 9. Those numbers are about this hardware, not about a product, and one of them has already produced BlarAI work — **#1587**, which is worth stating precisely because it is the opposite of a vindication. The appendix timed the TPM signer DIRECTLY. #1587 re-measured the SHIPPED verdict path and found the appendix pairing's software side wrong by 230x, the ratio a property of how full the chain was rather than of the system, and the quotable quantity the DELTA of ~90–135 ms. The design that figure appeared to argue for — a software chain with only its head hardware-signed — was then **declined**: per-record TPM signing STAYS (LA 2026-09-18, shipped `bcb8313d`), nothing is built and nothing merges dormant behind a flag for it. So the appendix earned its keep by provoking a measurement that overturned it, which is exactly why a probe record is kept rather than a probe conclusion.

5. **The study stands as merged and is NOT retracted.** It is a correct study of a moment, and a no-go is a study succeeding rather than a study wasted. It gains a superseded banner naming the outcome, so a reader who finds it learns the answer in the same place the recommendation was made.

6. **No BlarAI runtime behaviour was ever changed by this programme and nothing needs unwinding.** The study's own scope forbade it and that held. `C:/Users/mrbla/governator` is untouched and its disposition is the LA's — archive, keep privately, or delete on his word. No session works in it or carries anything out of it. Nothing was ever published, purchased, registered or applied for.

## Alternatives considered

- **Option A — defer rather than cancel, with a check-back on the next platform release.** **Rejected.** The LA recorded the outcome — not deferred, no check-back — rather than weighing this option; the reasoning below is mine. A deferral is a claim that the blocking condition is expected to change, and this one is not: the cooperative-seam finding is a property of how agents are built, not a gap awaiting a release. A check-back would have kept sixteen tickets alive, kept the repository in the constellation, and kept every session paying attention to a venture that is over. This project's own deferral discipline refuses a predicate that names no observable event, and no honest observable one exists here.
- **Option B — cancel the whole programme, capabilities included.** **Rejected.** This was my first draft of the cancellation record and it was wrong. Three of the four capabilities landed in BlarAI independently of the venture — #1545, #1544 and #1542 all merged on 2026-09-18, as BlarAI trust-spine work, on the day the venture was cancelled; the fourth set was filed only after each was measured **absent** from BlarAI. Cancelling them would have discarded BlarAI's own trust-spine work because a business decision went the other way. The correction was made the same day, before anything was built on it.
- **Option C — cancel the venture, retain the capabilities as BlarAI work, keep the measured appendices, and record the judgement rather than only the outcome. (CHOSEN.)**

## Consequences

- **No runtime change.** Byte-identical behaviour. This is a decision-record correction with a documentation footprint only.
- **The register stops misleading.** Its 2026-09-17 row is the decision SSOT for this programme and recorded a founded product with BlarAI as its first shadow target. It keeps that text, visible, under a superseded banner, with the correction as its own dated row — the wrong version stays readable so a reader can see what changed rather than only what is now claimed.
- **#1566 closes unbuilt.** It deferred a `<repo_constellation>` pointer to the new repository behind an observable predicate — CLAUDE.md under 47,800 bytes LF-normalised. **The predicate has since come true** (47,720 bytes at `fe865e12`) and the work is moot anyway, because there is nothing to point at. That is the deferral gate working as designed: the predicate was checkable, it was checked, and it decided something.
- **The attention cost is recovered.** The study measured the venture's ongoing demand at 8–14 hours a week for the specification path and effectively full time for a paid product, against a maintenance treadmill of roughly three semantics-changing changes a month on **one** agent's hook surface (61.6% of 281 releases in twelve months touched it). None of that is owed now.
- **What this record does not settle:** whether the cancellation was correct. The fact that argued the other way is recorded in Context rather than omitted; the judgement is the LA's.
