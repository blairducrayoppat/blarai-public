# Brief: feasibility study for runtime action governance as a product for Windows PCs

*Written 2026-09-17 for the session that runs the study (Vikunja #1549, whose description is the specification). This brief carries the full question list, the inventory of Windows seams to probe, the evidence standard, the method, and the decision points that come back to the Lead Architect (LA). It is an input to the study, not the study. The study's deliverable is `docs/research/runtime-action-governance-product-feasibility-<landing date>.md`.*

## The idea, in the LA's terms

An application that works with any AI model, local or cloud, on any device (Windows on Intel at least), and secures what AI agents do on the machine: it scans the device for the resources an agent could use, lets a person say which capabilities and permissions agents may have, and expresses those as universal profiles that govern any agent, abstracting the models and devices underneath. One place for AI governance for the average Windows PC owner. People do not trust AI; this builds that trust for them.

What the LA values as an outcome: revenue would be welcome; adoption by hundreds of thousands of people or more is also a good outcome, including the case where Microsoft adopts it. Both the commercial and the open-source paths are to be explored, and the deep-integration-with-Windows-11 path is required, because it is the way to attack the central limit found in the landscape work: a governor can only govern what passes through it.

## What the landscape work already established (read, do not re-derive)

`docs/research/runtime-action-governance-landscape-2026-09-16.md` scored 110 artefacts on five questions: action identity, approval binding, receipt, enforcement seam, local operation. The facts the study builds on:

- No surveyed product for a person's own machine exists that is local, deterministic and agent-agnostic. The one aimed at endpoints, Microsoft Defender for Endpoint's AI agent runtime protection, is enterprise-licensed, decides in the cloud, and covers four agents through their vendor hook interfaces plus network inspection.
- The strong technical pattern appears six times in the field and is small: a canonical action, a hash the approval binds to, re-derivation before execution, single use, a signed receipt. BlarAI has most of it in code with a Trusted Platform Module (TPM) signer behind it (`docs/governance/trust-spine-claim-register.md`, rows 1 to 13 and 27 to 29).
- Every in-process governance contract in the field says of itself that it is not a boundary against a host that skips it. This is the problem the deep-integration path exists to attack.
- Approval fatigue is documented by two vendors (auto-approve rising with experience; approvals made too fast to be meaningful). Profiles and bounded leases are the known answer; BlarAI's per-turn envelope is a working example.
- A canonical action vocabulary over categories (what kind of thing, what it touches, whether it is reversible, where the data goes) is what makes a profile agent-agnostic; tool names cannot (`docs/research/trust-spine-proposed-items-index.md`, item 4, #1545).
- The honest form of any coverage claim is a disclosure of what is governed and what is not (item 15, #1544).
- **A framing bias to correct for.** The five questions the compilation scored are close to the framing of the CAVA and PCAA papers (Ond Holdings), which the LA read before the rest of the field, so those papers and their peers score well on them by construction. Their design claims hold as running code; their quantitative claims cannot be checked from outside; their product is a hosted control plane reached through a client library, the shape this study excludes. The large vendors' strength, owning the enforcement seams and reaching hundreds of millions of machines, is under-weighted by the five questions and is scored here by Q1, Q2 and Q15 instead. The study borrows the vocabulary freely (it is becoming a commons: the CAVA core is Apache-2.0, the Agent Hooks contract MIT, the Agent Control Standard at OWASP) and bets on no company.

## What the product must include, at product altitude

The fifteen items on `docs/research/trust-spine-proposed-items-index.md` are BlarAI's hardening backlog, and most of them are the product's core features under BlarAI's implementation names. The study treats them at the altitude below, as capability requirements a person could read, not as mechanisms. The mechanics (hash algorithms, lease fields, record schemas, which function computes what) are design-time detail; the compilation's group D rows, the claim register and the index point to them, and the study cites a mechanism only where a feasibility judgment depends on it, for example whether a given Windows seam can supply the canonical action at all.

## The questions, with what "answered" looks like

**Q1. The seams on Windows 11.** For each seam below: what it can see (the action's meaning, or only bytes and destinations), what it can stop (before execution, or only observe), what escapes it, what it costs to use (privileges, drivers, signing, user consent), and whether it works on this machine today. Answered means a table with one row per seam and a recorded probe per row.

**Q2. The deep-integration path.** Where could a governor live so that agents cannot go around it, and what does each location require. Answered means a ranked list of integration routes with, for each, what only Microsoft can do, what a third party can do today, the programme or certificate that gates it, and its cost in money and time.

**Q3. Universal profiles.** The canonical action vocabulary, the profile format a non-technical person can read and edit, the device scan as an inventory, the approval and lease model. Answered means a draft vocabulary, three example profiles in plain language (a cautious home user; a developer running coding agents; a small business), and the mapping of at least two real agents' actions into the vocabulary.

**Q3a. The antivirus interaction model (LA direction, 2026-09-17).** The consumer template to study for approval fatigue is antivirus software: Bitdefender, Microsoft Defender and their peers. A person sets it up once, it then works in the background, and it interrupts only with a notification when something needs them. Study what those products do to avoid decision fatigue (quiet or autopilot modes, scoped "allow once / always" answers, exclusion lists, notification digests, a periodic report, a dashboard with an activity history) and decide what transfers to governing actions rather than files, and where it breaks: an action approval can bind money or data leaving the machine, so a wrong "allow" costs more than a quarantined false positive, and the product's own telemetry posture must be a design decision because a governor that phones home undermines the local-only promise. Answered means a description of the setup-then-background experience for each of the three example profiles, the events that may interrupt a person and the ones that must not, and the receipt history a person can open afterwards. The antivirus market is also the comparable for Q5 and Q6: a free platform baseline (Defender) reshaped a paid category, and the study should say what that history predicts for a governor, on both paths.

**Q4. Coverage disclosure as a product feature.** Answered means a sketch of the screen that tells a person what is governed, what is observed only, and what is out of reach, per installed agent, with the honest default when nothing is known.

**Q5. The commercial path.** Segments, willingness to pay, price points and models (one-time, subscription, freemium), distribution channels, support and liability of shipping a security product, competition and the timing risk that the platform vendor ships it natively. Answered means a one-page business shape per segment with the assumptions named and the evidence for each marked as measured, reported, or guessed.

**Q6. The open-source path.** Specification plus reference implementation; licence choices and precedents (Apache-2.0, MIT, open-core with a source-available tier as Pipelock and Mastra do); where a specification could live (the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, which now holds MCP and A2A; OWASP's GenAI project, which hosts the Agent Control Standard; an open organisation of the `responsibleai` kind); community routes through OpenVINO and Hugging Face; the four realistic shapes of Microsoft adoption (copies it, adopts the specification, partners, acquires) and what each would require of the project. Answered means a recommended structure with the trade-off against the commercial path stated plainly.

**Q7. The relationship to BlarAI.** What of the trust spine is extractable as a library; what the product's threat model is (other people's machines, cloud models when they choose them, agents the product does not control) and how it differs from BlarAI's; the separate repository and rulebook; what must never be shared (secrets, the LA's data, BlarAI's keys). Answered means a list of extractable components with their current file paths, and a one-paragraph statement of the boundary between the two projects.

**Q8. Go or no-go, and the first-year shape.** Answered means a recommendation with named alternatives, and if go: the first governed agent (Claude Code on this machine through its own hooks is the proposed first target, because it exposes a pre-tool hook, it is the agent that builds BlarAI, and governing the workshop's own tools is a demonstration no vendor can make), the minimum lovable product, the discipline carried over (dormant by default, ceremonies for posture, receipts for everything), and the measure of "adopted".

**Q9. Law, liability and regulation (added 2026-09-17).** The legal form needed before shipping a security product (a sole proprietor's exposure against a company's), warranty and disclaimer terms, consumer-protection rules in the LA's jurisdiction, privacy law applied to a product that inventories a machine and records agent actions even when nothing leaves it, the EU AI Act's human-oversight provisions as a source of demand, the EU Cyber Resilience Act's duties on products with digital elements (vulnerability handling, updates, a software bill of materials) if it is ever sold there, Microsoft Store and code-signing requirements, trademark clearance for a name, and whether BlarAI's own licence permits extracting its code into a product. Answered means a table of obligations with the trigger for each (selling, distributing free, open-sourcing, selling in the EU) and the cost or step each implies; nothing filed, nothing purchased.

**Q10. The governor as a target: its own threat model (added 2026-09-17).** Antivirus products are attacked because they run with privilege; a governor inherits that. Model at least: an injected agent routing around or impersonating the governor; malware presenting itself as an agent; a person socially engineered into approving; a tampered profile file; the update and code-signing channel; a compromised adapter; privilege escalation through the governor's own service; and who governs the governor (it must not be able to change its own policy or coverage without the person, which is BlarAI's self-governance rule). Answered means a threat table with, per threat, the control that answers it and the control that does not exist yet, plus what an independent security review and a disclosure process would cost before a first release.

**Q11. Maintenance, churn and support (added 2026-09-17).** Agents update weekly and their hook interfaces change; Windows feature updates move seams; adapters break silently. Answered means: how coverage drift is detected and shown (the coverage screen as a live instrument, not a static claim), a conformance suite per adapter, who maintains adapters on each path (a company, a community, both), a support model a one-person shop can carry, and how failures are learned about when telemetry is off by default (opt-in diagnostics the person can inspect before sending).

**Q12. Performance and resources (added 2026-09-17).** Overhead per governed action against the field's figures (sub-millisecond to tens of milliseconds), memory and battery on ordinary laptops, boot-time cost, the cost of hardware-key signatures at agent call rates (chain every record by hash, sign periodically), and Windows on ARM, which "Windows on Intel" leaves out. Answered means measured numbers on this machine for the seams that were probed, with their conditions, and a stated budget per action for the product.

**Q13. Privacy of the record, and shared machines (added 2026-09-17).** A history of what a person's agents did is sensitive even as labels and hashes. Retention, deletion, export, encryption of the history at rest, deletion on uninstall; multi-user PCs (profiles per person while the governor runs machine-wide), family machines, employer-owned machines, and consent when a record could describe someone else's use. Answered means a data map (what is stored, where, for how long, who can read it) and the multi-user design decision with its trade-off stated.

**Q14. Why anyone would trust the governor (added 2026-09-17).** Open source or reproducible builds, an independent review, a published disclosure process, the product shipping its own claim register (what it claims, the evidence, the limits, in the shape of `docs/governance/trust-spine-claim-register.md`), and the coverage screen as the product's contract. Answered means the trust artefacts a first release must carry and what each costs.

**Q15. Partners, timing, money and continuity (added 2026-09-17).** Whether agent vendors would keep their hook interfaces stable or list the product; whether antivirus vendors and Intel are partners or competitors; the signals that Microsoft is about to ship this natively and what to do when they appear; how demand would be validated cheaply before building (planned only; nothing is posted during the study); what the first year costs in money (a code-signing certificate, developer accounts, legal formation, a security review) and in the LA's attention against BlarAI; and continuity for users if the LA stops (the foundation route as insurance). Answered means a one-page first-year cost and attention estimate with its assumptions, and a continuity plan on each path.

**Q16. The first user (added 2026-09-17).** The LA is a novice user and the product's first test subject. Answered means a description of how the setup screen and the first approval prompt would be tested with him before any wider test, and what would count as a pass.

## The seam inventory to probe (hypotheses, every one to be verified on this machine)

Each entry names what is believed and where to look. Nothing here is established; the study establishes or refutes each with a probe recorded in the deliverable. Microsoft documentation is cited with its `ms.date`; announced features are checked for their current state as of the study's date, on Windows 11 Pro 10.0.26200.

1. **Agent-native hook interfaces.** Claude Code exposes `PreToolUse` (allow, deny, ask, defer, with `updatedInput`) and 32 other events; Codex CLI has approval policies and opt-in OpenTelemetry events; GitHub Copilot CLI and the GitHub Copilot app expose hook interfaces that Defender for Endpoint's agent runtime protection consumes. Probe: install nothing new that is not already present; for Claude Code, register a hook on this machine and observe a real tool call denied by it. Source: the Anthropic survey (`landscape-anthropic-agent-governance-2026-09-16.md`) and the Microsoft survey's Defender for Endpoint section.
2. **MCP clients through a local proxy.** Any agent that speaks the Model Context Protocol (MCP) can be pointed at a local gateway that sits between it and its servers; the field has a dozen such gateways (standards survey, group G of the compilation). Probe: whether an MCP client on this machine can be redirected without its cooperation, and what a proxy sees (tool name and arguments, in the clear, per call).
3. **Windows's own MCP support.** Microsoft announced native MCP support in Windows 11 in 2025: an MCP registry, App Actions, and a security architecture described as proxy-mediated communication, tool-level authorisation and runtime isolation. Verify what shipped, in which builds, and whether a third party can register as the authorising component or only as a server. This is the most important single item for the deep-integration path.
4. **The Windows agent workspace and agent accounts.** Microsoft announced, in late 2025, experimental agentic features in Windows 11 in which agents run under separate accounts with scoped access to a workspace. Verify current status on this build, what an agent account can and cannot reach, whether a third party can set or observe those scopes, and what a governor gains from them.
5. **Local model servers.** Ollama, Foundry Local, LM Studio, OpenVINO Model Server and similar expose local HTTP endpoints; an agent using one can have its tool calls observed in the model's output stream before the agent executes them. Probe: which servers are installed here, whether a local proxy in front of one sees tool-call intent, and whether that is a seam or only an observer.
6. **Network.** The Windows Filtering Platform (WFP) allows per-process filters; Windows Firewall allows per-application rules; DNS can be pinned. These govern destinations, never meanings, and cannot see inside TLS without interception the study should treat as out of bounds for a consumer product. Probe: a per-process outbound block on this machine, applied and observed.
7. **OS isolation.** AppContainer, Windows Sandbox, Win32 app isolation for packaged apps, Windows Defender Application Control (WDAC), Smart App Control and AppLocker constrain what a process can touch and what may run. Probe: whether an agent process can be launched under a constraint a governor sets, and what the constraint costs the agent's usefulness.
8. **Observation.** Event Tracing for Windows (ETW) providers for process, file and network events, and the Event Log, can record what an agent process did after the fact. This is the receipt seam when no gate exists. Probe: whether a user-mode consumer can capture the events a governor would need without a kernel driver.
9. **Identity and keys.** Windows Hello for presence, and Cryptography Next Generation (CNG) with TPM-backed keys for signing receipts, are already used by BlarAI (`shared/security/hello_verifier.py`, `shared/security/tpm_signer.py`). Probe: nothing new; confirm portability across machines (any Windows 11 PC with TPM 2.0).
10. **Security-product integration routes.** Registration with the Windows Security Center, the Microsoft Virus Initiative, Early Launch Anti-Malware and protected-process light, the Microsoft Store and its policies, and code-signing requirements. Read the programme terms and record which are open to an individual, which require a company, and what each costs. Do not apply to any programme.

## Evidence standard, method, and constraints

- **Documentation is not evidence.** A seam that is only read about is marked "reported"; a seam probed on this machine is marked "measured", with the command, the version, the date and the result recorded in the deliverable. Vendor prose is discounted against what the artefact shows.
- **Probes live outside the BlarAI tree.** Scratch scripts in the session's scratchpad or a throwaway directory; no code enters BlarAI's runtime; no BlarAI configuration changes; no new software installed unless the study's method needs it and it is uninstalled afterwards, recorded either way.
- **Nothing leaves the machine.** No external posting, no accounts created, no purchases, no programme applications, no contact with Microsoft or any foundation. Those are the LA's decisions and come back as [PROPOSED].
- **Fan out.** Subagents for the seam probes (one per seam group), the market and pricing research, the licensing and standards research; the compiling session re-derives every conclusion from what the subagents return, as the landscape compilation did, and states its own legend before scoring anything.
- **One report at the end.** Findings are banked on #1549 as dated CURRENT STATE comments at each seam, not narrated mid-flight in chat.
- **Author is not verifier.** An independent adversarial review of the deliverable before merge, with a disposition record for every finding (`scripts/verify_disposition.py` must pass).
- **Numbers carry their conditions.** Any timing or cost figure records what was running, on what build, on what date.
- **Plain language for the LA.** Every acronym expanded on first use; outcome first; the decision points at the top of the deliverable.

## What comes back to the LA, marked [PROPOSED] with a recommendation

1. Go or no-go, and whether the first year is a product, a specification, or both.
2. Open, commercial, or open-core, with the trade-off stated.
3. A name (the study may propose candidates; the choice is the LA's).
4. Whether to open a separate repository, and its rulebook.
5. Whether and how to approach Microsoft or a foundation, and when.
6. The legal form and the jurisdiction, if go (Q9), and the multi-user consent posture (Q13).
