## Outcome in three lines
1. The addressable Windows base is large and mostly Windows 11 (68.6% of desktop Windows worldwide, 71.3% in Europe, Aug 2026); Microsoft's own top-line is 1.6 billion monthly active Windows devices and 1 billion on Windows 11. The AI-agent developer segment is now measured in the low millions per tool (Codex >5M weekly, Claude Code "millions", Copilot 4.7M paid seats) and roughly half of professional developers run Windows, but no source splits agent users by OS.
2. Consumers pay $40–60 (EUR 40–50) first-year for a 3–5 device security suite that renews at $95–125 (EUR 80–110); free tiers dominate (61% of US antivirus users pay nothing; Avast converted ~4% of desktop users). Developers already spend $10–200/month on agent subscriptions, and the only per-app network governor with a one-time model (Little Snitch, $59) is macOS-only; every Windows equivalent is free/donation-ware or a cheap subscription.
3. Agent-security vendors either give the core away (Apache-2.0 / MIT) and charge $49–99/month for coordination or sell enterprise-only with no public price; the one public full ladder (Pipelock) runs free → $99/mo → $999/yr → $25,000/yr.

### C2. One-time vs subscription vs freemium
- Freemium conversion anchors: Avast ~4% of desktop users paid (2017/18, above); generic benchmark 1–5% broad-market, 5–15% targeted high-intent (https://www.withdaydream.com/library/insights/freemium-conversion-rate, 2026-04-09). Gen's 79M paid / 500M users = 16% but that pool includes acquired paid bases (LifeLock, MoneyLion) — I derived this ratio. Bitwarden and 1Password publish no conversion rates. NOT FOUND for Malwarebytes/AVG separately.
- Subscription migrations: Sketch moved from $99 one-time to $99/yr "updates licence" in June 2016, app keeps working without renewal (https://www.macstories.net/news/sketch-changes-direction-on-pricing/); Panic Nova (2020) copied it: $99 + $49/yr for updates (https://blog.panic.com/nova-our-next-big-thing/); 1Password went subscription-only ~2018 and ARR rose ~$20M (2017) → ~$60M (2019) on the team-seat pivot (https://sacra.com/c/1password/, secondary). Little Snitch has stayed one-time since the 2000s with paid major upgrades (v6: $59 new / $39 upgrade, 2024-05-21) — a 20-year demonstration that a per-app network governor can live on one-time pricing, on macOS.
- Recent price rises in trust products without visible churn data: Bitwarden Premium $10 → $19.80/yr (Jan 2026, first change in ~10 years; secondary), 1Password +33% Individual / +20% Families (2026-03-27; community pushback but grandfathering for legacy tiers, https://mjtsai.com/blog/2026/03/09/1passwords-price-hike/).
- Renewal shock is the norm in consumer AV: Norton renews at 2.4–2.8× first-year (US $49.99→$124.99; DE €39.99→€109.99); Bitdefender and ESET show only first-year prices; Malwarebytes Ultimate reportedly doubles at renewal (low confidence).

### C3. Developer willingness to pay for local safety/sandbox tools
- Direct evidence is thin. Found: Docker Desktop's paid gate (>250 employees or >$10M revenue; Pro $9/mo) shows developers/employers pay for a local runtime when licensing forces it — https://www.docker.com/pricing/faq/. Pipelock's public founding counter ("2 of 50 claimed" at $49/mo, fetched 2026-09-17) is the only live datapoint for an agent firewall aimed at individual developers — tiny. Objective-See (LuLu) runs as a donation-funded 501(c)(3) with public Form 990s (amounts not fetched); Sandboxie-Plus, TinyWall, simplewall run on Patreon/donations; Firejail is free GPL — i.e., every Windows-side per-app governor is unpaid. Little Snitch sales volumes are not public. NOT FOUND: any survey of developers' spend on sandboxing/egress tools.
- Bounding what developers already spend on agents (Section B): $10–39/mo (Copilot Pro/Pro+), $20–200/mo (Claude Pro/Max, Cursor Pro/Ultra, Warp Build/Max, ChatGPT Plus/Pro). A governor priced at $3–8/mo sits at 5–15% of that spend; a $59 one-time price matches Little Snitch exactly.
