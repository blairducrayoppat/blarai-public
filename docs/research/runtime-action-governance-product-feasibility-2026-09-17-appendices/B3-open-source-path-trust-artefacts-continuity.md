## 0. Outcome first — the recommended structure and its price

**Recommendation.** Split the work into three artefacts with three licences, and pick the home for each by what it must survive:

1. **The specification and its conformance suite: Apache-2.0 for code and schemas, CC BY-SA 4.0 for the prose**, contributions under the DCO (a sign-off, not a rights grant), in the founder's own GitHub organisation with `GOVERNANCE.md`, `SECURITY.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and a named succession clause from day one. This is exactly the licence pair and file set the OWASP Agent Control Standard ships today (`LICENSE` Apache-2.0, `LICENSE-DOCS`, `GOVERNANCE.md`, `project.owasp.yaml`; REPORTED, §2), and Apache-2.0 is where MCP itself is moving (its `LICENSE` file is a MIT-to-Apache-2.0 transition notice; REPORTED, §1.1). Year one, contribute the vocabulary into ACS's workstreams rather than founding a rival standard; ACS is free to join, already hosts the Microsoft AGT reference implementation, and is led by named individuals under OWASP's project process. Year three, if two or more independent implementations exist, propose the conformance suite to a foundation (§2 — completed from the foundations strand).
2. **The Windows governor's core: Apache-2.0**, so that every adapter author, every foundation, and every platform vendor can take it without asking, and so that a fork is legal the day the founder stops.
3. **The commercial tier (the parts a business pays for): the Functional Source License, FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0**, which converts each release to Apache-2.0 two years after it ships (REPORTED, fsl.software). Not ELv2 and not PolyForm Noncommercial: both are perpetual, neither has a change date, and a solo maintainer's disappearance would leave that code closed forever. Pipelock (Apache-2.0 core, ELv2 `enterprise/`) and Mastra (Apache-2.0 outside `ee/`, a proprietary "Enterprise Edition License v1.0" inside it) show the directory-split pattern works in this exact market (REPORTED, §1.1); the FSL variant adds the continuity guarantee they lack.

**The trade-off against the commercial path, stated plainly.** The commercial path is BlarAI's own structure: PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 plus a commercial licence held by the sole author. It keeps every right and every euro with the founder. It also closes three doors this study exists to open: no foundation will host it (Software Freedom Conservancy and OWASP require OSI-approved or DFSG-free licences; REPORTED, §2 and §6), no platform vendor adopts a specification whose only implementation is noncommercial-only (every specification Microsoft has adopted had a permissive reference implementation and more than one implementer; REPORTED, §4), and no fork is legal when the author stops (PolyForm has no change date; REPORTED, §1.2). The open structure above buys those three doors at one price: the core can be copied by anyone, including Microsoft. That price is real but it is not new — the AppGet precedent shows Microsoft re-implemented a permissively licensed design without needing the licence at all (§4a), so the licence never protected against shape (a); only dated public prior art, a community, and being the reference implementer do. What the founder gives up by going open is exclusivity on the core; what he keeps is the commercial tier, the trademark, the reference-implementer position, and the receipts of everything he shipped first.

**What this strand could not settle.** Whether the founder's country of residence allows individual Authenticode validation through Microsoft's Artifact Signing (individuals must be in the United States or Canada; REPORTED, §5.3); the list price of that service (the pricing page renders placeholders through the fetch tool); the primary CRA text (eur-lex returned nothing through the fetch tool, so the open-source-steward reading rests on OpenSSF's summary); and the copyrightability of agent-generated code, on which the entire "sole author can relicense" premise rests (§1.3).

### 1.3 The specific conflict: BlarAI (PolyForm NC + commercial licence) and an Apache-2.0 or MIT product

**Who holds BlarAI's copyright (MEASURED, read-only).** `git -C C:/Users/mrbla/BlarAI shortlog -sne --all` on 2026-09-17 lists seven identities: `Blair <mr.blair.do@gmail.com>` 4,075 commits; `Blair DuCray-Oppat <mr.blair.do@gmail.com>` 11; `blarai <blarai@local>` 5; `claude <claude@local>` 3; `Blair <blair@local>` 2; `Blair DuCray-Oppat, AIGP <mr.blair.do@gmail.com>` 2; `rev <r@r>` 1. The `@local` and `r@r` identities are this machine's own agent-produced commits (their subjects are BlarAI's own merges, ledger and journal entries, dated 2026-06 to 2026-08). There is no `CONTRIBUTING`, `CONTRIBUTORS`, `AUTHORS` or `NOTICE` file at the repo root, no CLA, and no `Copyright (c)` header naming anyone else in `shared/`, `services/` or `launcher/`. The `openvino/`, `optimum-intel/` and `.export-venv/` directories that carry third-party licence files are gitignored (`.gitignore` lines 31, 32, 103) and untracked (0 tracked files). Conclusion: **one natural-person author, no third-party contributor of record.**

**What the author must do to extract code into an Apache-2.0 or MIT product.** Nothing beyond choosing to. PolyForm NC binds licensees, not the licensor; its "No Other Rights" section says in terms that the licence does not "prevent the licensor from granting licenses to anyone else". The copyright holder may publish any of his own code under any other licence at any time, including alongside the existing PolyForm grant. The mechanics are: copy the files into the new repository, replace the `Required Notice:` header with an Apache-2.0 header and a `NOTICE` file, and stop there. No notice to existing PolyForm licensees is owed; they keep the grant they have.

**Does PolyForm NC on BlarAI create any obligation on the product?** No. PolyForm NC is not copyleft; it imposes no condition on works the licensor creates from his own code. The only obligations that could follow the code are (1) third-party code inside BlarAI under a licence that conflicts with Apache-2.0 — none was found by header scan (dependencies declared in requirements are used as dependencies, not vendored, and stay dependencies); and (2) the authorship question below.

**The premise this rests on (not verified, and outside what a web strand can settle).** Most of the 4,098 commits were produced with an AI coding agent under the author's direction, and three carry an agent identity. The commercial licence, the PolyForm grant, and any relicensing all rest on the author's claim to be the copyright owner of that output. Copyrightability of AI-assisted code is contested in several jurisdictions and settled in none this strand read; the founder should treat "sole author can relicense" as a claim the product's legal review must confirm, not as a fact this report established.

**The reverse direction: pulling an open governor back into BlarAI.** Apache-2.0 code may be included in a PolyForm-NC-licensed tree: Apache-2.0 is not copyleft and PolyForm NC does not forbid mixing; BlarAI would carry the governor's `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` and mark modified files (§4). Two consequences follow, and they are the real decision:

- If the open governor accepts outside contributions under the DCO (a sign-off that certifies origin and grants nothing — developercertificate.org), those contributions arrive in BlarAI **only** under Apache-2.0. The founder cannot convert them to PolyForm NC or to his commercial licence; he may only ship them under Apache-2.0 inside a mixed tree. That is harmless for a product that is itself open-core.
- If the founder wants to keep the right to relicense everything (the BlarAI posture), he needs a CLA with a sublicensing grant. HashiCorp's CLA grants "a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable copyright license" and the right to sublicense (hashicorp.com/cla); that grant is what made the 2023 MPL-to-BSL switch legally possible without asking contributors — and what made the OpenTofu fork the community's answer. Elastic's 2021 move to SSPL/ELv2 produced the OpenSearch fork; Elastic added AGPL-3.0 back on 2024-08-29 once "Amazon is fully invested in their fork" (elastic.co/blog/elasticsearch-is-open-source-again). A CLA is therefore both the tool of the commercial path and the field's best-known trust cost.
- The MCP repository shows the cost of relicensing **without** a CLA even between two permissive licences: contributions whose authors have not consented "remain licensed under the MIT License" (LICENSE, 4a939857). Decide the inbound terms before the first outside pull request, not after.

**Precedents in this exact market for the directory split**: Pipelock (Apache-2.0 + ELv2 `enterprise/`), Mastra (Apache-2.0 + EE `ee/`), and the OWASP ACS's `LICENSING.md`/`LICENSE-DOCS` pair for code versus prose (all REPORTED, §1.1).

## 2. Where a specification could live

Two homes were read directly in this strand; the full comparison (Agentic AI Foundation, OWASP GenAI Security Project, the `responsibleai` pattern, OpenSSF, Eclipse, Apache Incubator, IETF/W3C/LF Projects) is in §2.3, from the foundations strand.

### 2.1 What was read directly

**OWASP Agent Control Standard (GenAI-Security-Project/agent-control-standard).** `GOVERNANCE.md` (branch `integration`): project lead Rock Lambros; creators Michael Bargury and Ory Segal remain project leaders; five workstreams with named leads; triage on a weekly call via `scope:`/`priority:`/`workstream:` labels; maintainers are added when "existing leads propose additions and removals" and the project lead confirms, by a pull request that updates the governance file, `project.owasp.yaml` and `CODEOWNERS` together. Licences: Apache-2.0 code, CC BY-SA 4.0 docs. README: pull requests fork from and target `integration`; reference implementation runs Microsoft's AGT. Cost to contribute: none; OWASP membership is not required to contribute to a project. What a solo founder gets: a live, Microsoft-adjacent venue with weekly triage where a vocabulary contribution (a Windows-seam profile, a receipt format, a coverage-disclosure schema) can land as a workstream item rather than as a rival standard. What he gives up: the ACS name and roadmap belong to its leads; his contribution is Apache-2.0/CC BY-SA the moment it is merged.

**The `responsibleai` organisation.** Created 2019-07-24, five public repositories (ASSERT 246 stars; agent-hooks 13; agent-control-spec 3; assert-ai-action; assert-ci-banking-demo), blog `aka.ms/responsible-ai-governance`. Microsoft's Command Line blog (2026-08-27; authors Mohammad Abuomar, Caitie McCaffrey, Sarah Bird) says the specs and SDKs are "MIT-licensed under an open organization" so that "no single runtime defines the contract", and names two certified implementations (the independent ACS policy runtime and Microsoft Agent Framework). The `agent-hooks` licence file's copyright line is "Microsoft Corporation"; the crate's is "responsibleai". `GOVERNANCE.md` names a single maintainer with decision authority and a succession clause ("ownership transfers within the `responsibleai` GitHub organization"). Read plainly: this is a Microsoft-authored contract placed in a neutral-looking namespace with one Microsoft-employed maintainer and no external governance body — a pattern a solo founder can copy for year one (own org, `GOVERNANCE.md`, succession clause), and a warning that "open organization" is a label, not a structure.

**AAIF, as visible from the artefacts.** goose now lives at `github.com/aaif-goose/goose` (the `block/goose` URL resolves there; 54,369 stars; no `parent` field, i.e. a transfer) and the MCP spec repo carries a formal MIT-to-Apache-2.0 relicensing notice — both consistent with Linux Foundation project intake norms (Apache-2.0 preferred, a foundation-owned organisation). The charter, fees and proposal process are in §2.3.

### 2.2 What the choice turns on for a solo maintainer

Every foundation route trades control for continuity, and every one of them presumes something a solo project does not yet have: a second maintainer, an existing user base, or an independent implementation. The Apache Incubator requires a champion, mentors and an initial committer list; OpenSSF and AAIF intake at the sandbox level still asks who else depends on the project; Software Freedom Conservancy "does not move rapidly to accept new projects" and prefers "mature communities with existing track records", takes 10% of processed revenue, and can take "many months" (sfconservancy.org/projects/apply). The honest year-one answer is therefore not a foundation: it is a well-governed personal organisation plus contribution into ACS, with the foundation move reserved for the point where the project has the thing foundations want — implementers other than its author.

### 5.2 The ordered list for a first release

*Free and days of work:* `SECURITY.md` + `security.txt` + private vulnerability reporting; SBOM in the release; GitHub attestations (SLSA L2) + cosign signatures; Scorecard action; Best Practices "passing"; the claim register and the coverage screen shipped in the product.
*Cheap and weeks:* Authenticode signing (Artifact Signing if the founder's residence permits individual validation, otherwise a CA-issued OV token); a Microsoft Store listing; reproducible pre-signature builds with the residual (signing, packaging) stated.
*Not in year one:* a paid independent audit; the Best Practices silver/gold badges; any kernel-mode component.

GUESSED totals for year one: cash in the low hundreds of USD (the signing subscription plus a domain) if Artifact Signing is available; a few hundred more for a CA token if not; founder time on the order of four to six weeks spread across the year, of which the claim register is the largest single item.

### 5.3 The residence question, stated because it is decisive

Artifact Signing's Public Trust individual validation is limited to individuals located in the United States or Canada (quickstart, ms.date 2026-05-21). The Lead Architect changes time zone every few weeks and his country of residence is not on record in this strand. If he is not resident in either country, individual Authenticode via Microsoft is closed to him and the choice is a CA-issued OV certificate or a company entity in a listed country. This is a fact to confirm, not a technical decision.

### 6.3 The year-one continuity kit

Six artefacts, all free: (1) Apache-2.0 on the core and FSL on the paid tier; (2) `GOVERNANCE.md` with a named second owner and a succession clause; (3) a GitHub successor set on the personal account and a second owner on the organisation; (4) release signing bound to the organisation's identity (Sigstore keyless + attestations), with Authenticode under whichever identity the residence rule allows; (5) a `CONTINUITY.md` that lists the install names (winget id, Store id, PyPI/crates names) and who may claim them; (6) the dead-man's clause. The precedent that best shows why is xz: a solo maintainer with no plan accepted the successor the attacker chose; the plan's purpose is to make the successor a choice made in calm, in public, before it is needed.

## 7. The recommended structure, restated with the trade-off

- **Spec + conformance suite:** Apache-2.0 / CC BY-SA 4.0, DCO, own org with governance files; contribute into OWASP ACS in year one; foundation proposal only with a second implementer.
- **Governor core:** Apache-2.0.
- **Commercial tier:** FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0 in a separate directory (the Pipelock/Mastra split), converting to Apache-2.0 two years after each release.
- **BlarAI:** unchanged (PolyForm NC + commercial). Code flows BlarAI → governor freely (sole author); governor → BlarAI freely for the founder's own code, and under Apache-2.0 only for anyone else's.
- **Trust artefacts at first release:** the free set in §5.2 plus Authenticode; the claim register and coverage screen as the product's contract.
- **Continuity:** the six-item kit in §6.3.

**Trade-off against the commercial path, once more and plainly:** the commercial path keeps exclusivity on everything and forecloses foundation hosting, vendor adoption of the spec, and a legal fork; the open path forfeits exclusivity on the core and keeps the other three. The AppGet record says exclusivity on a permissively licensed design was never protection against Microsoft anyway; PolyForm NC would not have changed winget. What protects a solo founder is being first, dated, public, and the reference — and that is exactly what the open path builds.
