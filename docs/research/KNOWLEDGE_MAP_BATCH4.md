## Card I. The agent-governance landscape and the runtime-action-governance venture

### Summary (5 lines)
1. In September 2026 a set of studies surveyed 110 products, papers and specifications on one question: when an AI agent is about to act, who decides, what does the approval attach to, and what record survives. No surveyed vendor ships all five properties the studies scored; BlarAI's perimeter (no network door except by ceremony, signed model files, born-encrypted storage) is stronger than anything surveyed, and its inner receipt (what a "yes" is bound to) is weaker than the best small open-source work.
2. Decided early: adopt no external governance software (not Microsoft's Agent Framework, not its Agent Governance Toolkit, not a hosted control plane). Borrow ideas only; 15 numbered "[PROPOSED]" hardening items sit in one list (`docs/research/trust-spine-proposed-items-index.md`), each owned by a ticket.
3. The venture to sell this as a product ("The Governator") went: study on 2026-09-17 recommending GO, cancelled by the LA 2026-09-18 (ADR-042), reopened 2026-10-05 on a narrower claim after the account-level network block was measured (ADR-049). Nothing is built; its own repository holds records only.
4. The 2026-10-11 off-the-shelf study concluded no product or combination meets the whole requirement set; the layers that make BlarAI trustworthy are not available off the shelf; the Governator is the project most exposed to Microsoft's open-source toolkit.
5. Not verified by me: any ticket's current state, the live content of the Vikunja Governator board, and the raw files of the five off-the-shelf studies beyond the final report (existence only).

### (a) The question
What does the rest of the world already do about governing what an AI agent may do at runtime, where does BlarAI stand against it, which ideas should BlarAI borrow, and is there a product worth building on top of it?

### (d) Layers of zoom
1. **Why this subject at all.** BlarAI's rule is that one door adjudicates every risky action and every other door is welded shut. The question after a year of building is whether others have a better door, a better receipt, or a reason to join up.
2. **The five questions** (landscape, "The name and the five questions"): what is the action; what does a human yes attach to; what record survives; where is the decision enforced; does it run on one machine with no vendor call.
3. **Where BlarAI stands.** Strong: the network guard, the single exempted importer, signed weight manifests, born-encrypted stores, hash-chained audit with a security-chip signer. Weak: the approval attaches to a tool name and rule label (Gap A), the two live doors write log lines rather than signed receipts (Gap B), the two egress layers agree only while one adjudicator holds the door slot, checked once at boot (Gap C), and the audit chain's own verifier is not called on the live path (Gap D) (index, "Where the four gaps are").
4. **The fact the AGT verdict exposes.** The Policy Agent service boots and receives no traffic; ADR-041 recorded this on 2026-07-15 as a decision (the socket path was designed for a per-agent-VM topology this hardware cannot have). The thing that adjudicates is `DeterministicPolicyChecker.check`, imported and called directly.
5. **What was taken from outside.** Hash a canonical form of the action, hand the hash to the approver, re-derive before execution, refuse on mismatch, consume once (six independent projects do this); escalate as a deny that a callback may lift; verify the audit chain on load; a decision-source field; a lock that approval text arriving inside untrusted content never lifts an escalation; the six-question Auditability Card (`docs/governance/auditability-card.md`).
6. **The venture, in order.** Concept spec and brief (2026-09-17) -> feasibility study (GO) -> LA takes three of six decisions and founds a separate repository -> LA cancels the next day with his reason recorded -> capabilities continue inside BlarAI -> LA reopens on 2026-10-05 after #1671 and #1689 -> scoping gate (#1693) -> off-the-shelf study on 2026-10-11 asks whether any of it should be bought instead.
7. **Smallest detail.** Appendix timing: ADR-042 Decision 4 records that #1587 re-measured the shipped signing path and found the appendix pairing's software side wrong by 230 times, "the opposite of a vindication", a reason the appendix numbers carry their conditions.

## Card J. Models and the upgrade watch

### Summary (5 lines)
1. BlarAI's resident brain is Qwen3-14B (4-bit, "INT4") on the Intel Arc 140V graphics chip, sped up by a small pruned Qwen3-0.6B "draft" model (speculative decoding, about 2 times throughput). All language-model work runs on the graphics chip (ADR-011, ADR-012). `brain_model_dir` in `default.toml` reads empty today, which means the compiled-in 14B (S).
2. The rule since 2026-06-02: Qwen3-14B stays. Candidates (Gemma 4 12B, Qwen3.6-27B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5 small models) have each been measured or read, and none has been adopted. The brain switch exists in code, dormant, and flipping it is an LA-present decision (a capability and quality change plus an ADR-012 amendment).
3. The strongest candidate is the 35B-A3B (a "mixture of experts" model of which about 3 billion parameters are active per word): 2.4 times faster than the 14B with its draft in the 2026-09-22 measurement, but it gave different pass counts on identical repeated runs of the agentic test suite (30, 28, 28 of 30) where the 14B gave 30 of 30 three times. The "it cannot switch off its reasoning" blocker was retracted the same day: the test caused it.
4. The watch's own trigger fired on 2026-09-17 when OpenVINO 2026.4 shipped the speed-up paths the watch named. The first condition for measuring it, the graphics-memory budget, reads 87 percent on this machine today (M).
5. Not verified by me: whether the 35B weights are still on disk at the path the log cites (the path was not found today); any ticket state; the content of the Hugging Face dataset; the 2026.4 side install.

### (d) Layers of zoom
1. **Why this card.** Every finding in the other cards is a finding about this one machine. Memory is the scarcest resource, so a big job (a 35B load, a high-resolution image refine) must evict and lazily reload the resident 14B.
2. **The box.** Lunar Lake laptop; the graphics chip carves its share from system memory; the share is a registry percentage that display-driver installs have reset three times in three weeks (August to September).
3. **The virtual machine.** One Hyper-V Generation 2 machine, Alpine Linux guest, dynamic memory, reached by the host over a virtual socket (AF_HYPERV) with no network stack; it holds the clean-room test runner (live) and the HTML parser (built, dormant).
4. **Topology.** Host mode: the assistant is a host process with the graphics chip; the launcher starts the machine; services in the machine are not the default path.
5. **Accounting.** Memory is measured as In-Use = Total - Available, never as a sum of process sizes; kernel pool, hypervisor-held memory and the graphics share are the hidden buckets.
6. **Measurement conditions.** Cold thermal gate, reboot-first for speed arms, quiet box, assistant down, unique conditions stamped on every row; a figure taken while the operator works is contaminated.
7. **Ceremonies.** Flip order and the per-capability runbooks; the LA is present; proven features in already-approved capabilities ship live, new capabilities and posture changes ship dormant and wait.
8. **Scheduled and unattended work.** Only elevated scheduled tasks, because a non-elevated launcher stalls on a permission prompt; large background GPU jobs are detached.
9. **Smallest detail.** The graphics-memory budget key that OpenVINO reports is not the real one; the registry key above is; the reported key was 87 while the real one sat at 57, "the instrument lies".
