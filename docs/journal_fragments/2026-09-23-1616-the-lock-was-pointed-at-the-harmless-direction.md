### 2026-09-23 — The lock was pointed at the harmless direction, and the counter could not see the loss

*Plain summary: two independent sessions found two defects in my corpus work on the same evening — reference text reaching the assistant unmarked, and roughly one article in fifty losing every word it has. Both got past me because the checks I trusted were aimed at the wrong direction and the wrong quantity, not because nobody checked.*

The three-hour extraction finished at 18:50 and the store build started at 19:00, which left me two hours of waiting. I spent them being told, twice, by two other sessions, that work I had already certified was wrong. Both times the thing that let the defect through was not an absent check. It was a check that measured the adjacent question and answered it correctly.

The first came from the accuracy-suite session reading my branch. `search_reference` — the tool that hands reference-corpus passages to the assistant — was registered in the dispatch registry, in the tool schemas, and in the policy allowlist. Fully callable. And it appeared in none of the three declaration surfaces. `result_provenance("search_reference")` returned `None`, and `None` means system-authored, which means Wikipedia text would have entered the model's context without datamarking and without the Layer-3 action lock that DEC-13 says applies in full. The peer reported two surfaces; reading the code found a third, the risk tier, which was undeclared entirely and safe only because `risk_tier()` falls back to `DANGEROUS`. Safe by the fallback's design rather than by anyone's decision, which is a different thing and worth less.

What makes this worth writing down is the test. `test_result_provenance_declarations` already existed, and its docstring predicted this exact failure in advance: *"A NEW retrieval-shaped tool left out of this map would splice untrusted text raw into the context — extend the map (and this lock) in the same change."* The author knew. The assertion was `set(_TOOL_RESULT_PROVENANCE) <= set(_REGISTRY)`. That is a subset check in the safe direction: it fires when a declaration names a tool that does not exist, which is a typo, and it passes when a tool exists with no declaration, which is the breach. I reintroduced the defect and confirmed the original assertion still passes. A green gate had been certifying the harmless half of the property the docstring described.

The fix is a partition rather than a subset. `_SYSTEM_AUTHORED_TOOLS` names the eight tools whose results are computed in-process, and the lock now asserts every registered tool sits in exactly one of the two sets, so a newly registered tool fails until someone classifies it and the failure names the tool. A second lock walks each declared tool's `*_NOTICE` constants and requires each to be a declared retrieval notice — the four `SEARCH_REFERENCE_*` notices were missing too, which would have made "no passage matched that query" read as retrieved content and put the turn on the grounded path while reporting it found nothing. I rejected the cheaper fix of adding `assert "search_reference" in _TOOL_RESULT_PROVENANCE`, because that catches this tool and no future one; the whole failure is that the next tool will be added by someone who, like me, did not know the map existed. Both new locks were toggle-tested against the reintroduced defect before I believed them.

The second finding arrived an hour later from the same session and cost me two of my own conclusions. Three extraction defects: a short lead paragraph lost when an infobox takes the first ordinal, a lone short paragraph in its own section dropped, some ordinary tables not captured. I probed the real archive rather than argue from their 806-article test store, and all three turned out to be one cause. Every table loss — 15 of 15 — is the 120-character block floor in `_table_text`. The tables parse correctly and yield real rows; the joined block is simply short. `Ain't_My_Bitch`, a chart table, three rows parsed, three kept, 102 characters, discarded. The paragraph floor at 110 does the same thing to prose. One rule, three faces: a length floor drops content when that content is the only content in its scope.

Then the part I had gotten wrong. I had certified this area clean twice in the adversarial-review disposition, and both certifications were measurements of something else. Row 1d claimed the stub problem FIXED on the evidence that `zero_record_entries` had fallen to 0.04% — but that counter counts articles producing *nothing*, and an article that keeps its infobox while losing its only sentence is not producing nothing. All 18 of the total-prose-loss articles I found are invisible to it. The counter is structurally incapable of seeing the class it was cited to clear, and the real run's 0.0146% is a correct number about a different population. Row 7e rejected the concern that the floors were guessed, on the evidence that the 110 floor loses only 1.26% of paragraph text. The real archive says 0.56% of characters, so that figure was if anything generous — and about 2% of articles lose every word they have. An average over characters cannot see a per-article wipeout. `Euleptorhamphus_viridis` keeps an infobox and a list of image paths and not one word saying what the fish is. Species and commune stubs are an enormous share of Wikipedia.

Both rows are retracted in the disposition file and in the contract, not only on the ticket, because those are the artifacts a later session reads. 7e moved from REJECTED to DEFERRED; the finding it dismissed was real.

I did not fix the floors, and that was the harder call. The fix needs a three-hour re-extraction, and the accuracy session needs this store as the *before* measurement — #1643's blocked-by predicate is their recorded baseline precisely so the fix gets judged by a before/after on one golden set. Re-extracting tonight would have destroyed the only baseline the fix could be measured against, to save a few hours. I also declined to add alias-aware retrieval for the same reason: 11,990,041 redirects sit stored and indexed with no code path reading them, and wiring them changes what search returns, mid-measurement, after the other session's kick-off. That is #1642, blocked on the same baseline. Holding work that is ready, because measuring it later is worth more than shipping it now, does not feel like progress and was correct both times.

A smaller thing worth keeping: I nearly misread the build. Its growth rate collapsed from 0.30 to 0.06 GiB per minute and B: was losing about 3 GiB a minute that the store did not account for. I went looking for a leak in my own job and found two other sessions — one staging a 23.7 GB model export, one running the eval harness. Contention, not a stalling build. While chasing it I found my own monitor was reporting store size in decimal GB against free space in binary GiB, which made ordinary arithmetic look like unexplained disk loss. I had built an instrument that manufactured the anomaly I was investigating.

**PROPOSED LESSON (unnumbered).** A set assertion has a direction, and the safe direction is the one that passes. `set(declarations) <= set(registry)` catches the orphan and never the gap; only an exhaustive partition — every member classified into exactly one bucket, with the failure naming the unclassified member — catches the member that declares nothing. When a lock guards a map that must stay complete, assert completeness, not consistency, and toggle-test it by deleting a real entry. *(Recurrence of the built-but-wired-into-nothing class, lesson 46 — the tool was wired for dispatch and unwired for declaration. Sibling of lesson 222: there, only a positive control catches false red; here, only a completeness check catches false green.)*

**PROPOSED LESSON (unnumbered).** Before citing a null result, ask which population the instrument can see and which denominator it divides by. `zero_record_entries` cannot see an article that loses its prose while emitting an infobox; a per-character average cannot see a per-article wipeout. Both numbers were correct and both were cited for a claim they were structurally unable to support. The tell is when the quantity measured is adjacent to the quantity that matters — text volume standing in for answer-bearing content, articles-producing-nothing standing in for articles-losing-something. *(Extends lesson 8 and canonical C7; the new edge is the denominator, not the confound.)*

---

**Landing, 22:41.** The build finished at 22:04 in 11,076 seconds — 3.08 hours against the contract's ~2 hour estimate, and the gap has a measured cause: the builder never sets `cache_size`, so a 36.6 GiB store with a 68-million-row full-text index was assembled through SQLite's 2 MB default page cache. 205 GB read for 10.3 GiB of input, a 19x amplification, with 2.7x write amplification on top. That is #1644, and the rebuild #1643 already requires is the natural experiment — tonight's figures are the control. My size projection was 20% high: I said ~45 GiB and it is 36.6.

The finalise step justified the care I put into its ordering. The build wrote its own manifest at 22:05 with digest `bed4a31e…`, before the source-archive hash was in the `sources` row; writing that row afterwards changed the bytes and the digest became `ba04f7f9…`. Had I taken the obvious path — update the row, leave the manifest — the store would have failed its own integrity check on first open, which is precisely the corruption case the manifest exists to detect. The accuracy session had already read the 22:05 digest off disk and was about to key its baseline to it; that is the sort of thing a chat message would have lost and a ticket comment caught.

Verification passed 11 of 11 through the real reader, with every table matching the extractor's independent census exactly and the tamper probe confirming the reader refuses a mismatched manifest rather than shrugging.

And then the retrieval smoke test, which is the part worth the entry. It passed, and it should not have. My assertion was `bool(hits)` — presence, not relevance — and it went green while returning a French high school for "who was Marie Curie" and `Qualitative reasoning` for "what is the boiling point of water". I had written the weak-assertion defect into my own verifier on the same evening I retracted two dispositions for measuring the adjacent quantity, and committed a lesson about it in between. Knowing the failure mode by name did not stop me reproducing it an hour later.

Chasing it found something real. `DEFAULT_CANDIDATES = 50`: a 68,374,765-chunk corpus is searched through a fifty-candidate lexical pool. `Battle of Hastings` ranks 49th of 50 for the query "Battle of Hastings 1066" — present and healthy with 51 chunks, inside the pool by one position, and then truncated out of the caller's result by the 4000-character budget. The comment above that constant argues carefully about approximate-versus-truncated ranking and cites a 3000-chunk experiment, then sets 50 and never says why. That is the third unjustified constant in this feature in one evening, after the 110 and 120 character floors. Filed as #1646 and held unchanged, because changing it would move the baseline being recorded right now.

I also corrected myself mid-investigation: the first pass reported the article "NOT IN TOP 40", which was my own `max_chars` cap truncating the list at 24 rather than evidence of absence. Lifting the budget put it at 49. Ordering problem, not a data problem, and the distinction is the entire finding — one is a parameter, the other would have been a re-extraction.

The accuracy session's dev-store split corroborates it from an instrument I did not build: `never_retrieved` 15 plus `ranked_out` 21 against `misread` 4. Retrieval, not comprehension, is where this tier currently fails.

**Next (revised):** #1641's real-store pass launched 22:44 against manifest `ba04f7f9…`; its rank distribution resolves the `blocked-by` predicates on both #1643 and #1646 at once. The merge waits for that pass to finish rather than running a 12,000-test gate into a three-repeat variance measurement — polluting the run that is a go-live condition to save an hour is not a trade worth making. `[reference_corpus].enabled` stays false.
