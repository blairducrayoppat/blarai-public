### 2026-08-06 — The gates were all green because none of them were reading the page

*Plain summary: a website is mostly words, and nothing in the system had ever asked for any of them — so I built the one part of that a machine can honestly check, and found the fleet's own static-site template shipping "Page title goes here".*

I picked up #1281 expecting to write a lint. What I actually spent most of the day on was working out where the line runs between a thing a machine can decide and a thing only the Lead Architect can, because that line is the whole ticket and getting it wrong in either direction produces something worse than nothing.

The gap #1281 names is not a grader missing something. It is a category the graders do not have. Trace a website end to end through this system and nothing asks what the site should SAY: the interview asks about platform, surface, features and assets; the plan carries `asset_specs` for images and has no equivalent for copy; the acceptance criteria grade behaviour — a function returns the right value, a page loads without a console error. So the coder invents filler, and every gate passes, because every gate is measuring something else. My scope was part 3 of three: the criterion the exams can actually check. Parts 1 and 2 — asking the operator what the site should say, and using his own words verbatim — are intake work and were explicitly not mine today.

The temptation, and I want it recorded because I felt it clearly, was to build a quality scorer. A readability metric, a tone check, a small model judging whether the prose is any good. All of that would have been easy to write, impossible to defend, and would have handed the operator a number that meant nothing. Whether the writing is GOOD is judgment and belongs to him (#1280). What a machine can decide is narrower and duller: the difference between content that was asked for and written, and placeholder nobody noticed. Text that announces itself as unfinished. I held to that line and the module says so in its own docstring, including the limit — bland but genuine copy is invisible to this scan and always will be, because there IS no textual difference between "we are passionate about quality" written in earnest and written to fill space.

The design decision I am least sure was obvious, and most sure was right, is that the scan PARSES the page instead of grepping it. The trap is `<input placeholder="you@example.com">`. That is not placeholder prose; that is a form hint, and it is the deliverable getting it RIGHT — the attribute exists to hold example text. A raw text search would have convicted the page that got it right and left the one that shipped lorem ipsum in a paragraph looking identical. So HTML pages go through `html.parser` and only their user-visible text is graded: element text plus `alt` and `aria-label`, which a screen-reader user hears as the page's words. `placeholder=`, `value=` and every other attribute are deliberately not read. Component and template files (`.jsx`, `.vue`, `.svelte`) cannot be parsed that way, so they get the raw scan restricted to the rules that survive losing context — "lorem ipsum" is filler wherever it sits, but a template phone number in raw source might be a form hint and is not graded there. That asymmetry is the same shape as the security scan's build-output split, and I stole it deliberately: the tier is chosen by which question the rule can still answer once the context is gone.

The thing I did not expect was a fourth state. Its sibling `SecurityResult` has three — unmeasured, clean, findings — and I started with the same. Then I ran it over a Python library and it reported "clean (0 pages scanned)", which is this scan claiming to have approved words it never read. A night of libraries would have rendered identically to a night of well-written sites. So `ProseResult` carries `applicable` alongside `measured`, and every consumer branches on `disposition()`, which returns one of four constants and cannot be recombined wrongly by a caller in a hurry. That is #1223's born-unmeasured invariant taken one step further: the registry's rule is that an instrument must tell "found nothing" from "did not look", and this one also has to tell both of those from "there was nothing here to look at". The rollup counts all four separately and the KPI line renders `no-prose-surface=N` even at zero, because "placeholder=0" on a night of five libraries and "placeholder=0" on a night of five websites are different facts and the operator reads one line.

I made the band contribution deliberately quieter than security's and I want the reasoning on the record rather than only in the docstring. An unambiguous finding is a B caveat — which is #1281's own stated predicate, that a delivery shipping lorem ipsum is reported rather than banded A — and never a C. The cost asymmetry that earns the security scan its C runs the other way here: a false clean there hands a non-technical operator a live credential, while a page of filler costs him a reread. The MEDIUM tier (a scaffold's default `<title>`, template contact details, framework demo strings) is recorded, stamped and counted but moves nothing at all, because each of those is conceivably deliberate and a control that convicts a real choice is a control that gets muted. And — this is the one that inverts the sibling rule — an UNMEASURED prose scan does not move the band either. A tree unreadable enough to blind this scan blinds every Layer-1 lint too, and spending the operator's single caveat slot on "the wording check could not run" would buy noise at the price of the finding he can act on. It is still stamped, still counted, still rendered. It just does not take a letter.

Then the part that made the day worth it. I ran the finished scan over the fleet's real substrate rather than only my own fixtures, because a lint proven against a defect its own author planted proves the regex and not the instrument. Thirteen real trees: four archived B5 web deliverables and the nine build-infra seeds. Six non-web seeds correctly reported not-applicable, the `web` and `winui` seeds came back clean, and every archived deliverable came back clean — those coders did write their own words. Zero false positives across the lot. And the `web-static` seed, the starting point for exactly the multi-page static site the Lead Architect asked for, ships this:

```html
<h1 class="placeholder">Page title goes here</h1>
<p class="placeholder">Replace this paragraph with the real introduction the goal asks for.</p>
```

Nobody planted that. It is the template doing its job, and a deliverable that wires up the layout and never replaces the words ships it verbatim — and would have banded A. It is now a seed-proof test, and it is the strongest evidence in the suite precisely because I did not write it.

The trade-off I rejected: I considered making the scan a hard gate that fails a delivery carrying placeholder prose. That would have been a capability change on day one of a category nothing has ever graded, on a rule set with no corpus behind it, and it is not a builder's call. Advisory, with its numbers visible on every surface — sidecar, evidence stamp, band reason, card, rollup, KPI line — is what lands; promoting it is a decision with an owner. The `disclosure_rate` in the instrument registry is registered as `RATE_UNMEASURED` for the same reason: I have not swept a corpus, and a plausible number nobody measured is exactly what that registry exists to make impossible.

Measured: standing gate 10224 passed / 0 failed / 0 skipped / 129 deselected on the branch, +43 over the 10179 baseline (35 in the new suite, 3 registry-named toggle/seam/quiet cases, and 7 from the parametrised registry sweep gaining one row). The baseline surfaces are deliberately NOT touched here — per `TEST_GOVERNANCE` §3 that belongs to the merging session, and with several builders in flight tonight a branch that bumps the figure just guarantees a conflict over a number that will be wrong anyway.

**Proposed lesson:** when a new grader has a state its closest sibling does not, copying the sibling's result shape is the failure mode, not the shortcut. `SecurityResult` has three states and I inherited all three before noticing that "there is nothing here to grade" is a fourth one my check has and its model does not — and that collapsing it into "clean" would have made a Python library and a finished website report identically. The sibling's shape encodes the sibling's question. Ask what states YOUR question really has before reusing it.

**Next:** #1281 parts 1 and 2 — the intake asking what the site should say, and the operator's own words used verbatim — are still open and are where the ceiling actually moves; this floor only catches the words nobody wrote. The seed's placeholder copy is not a defect in the seed (a template SHOULD say "replace this"); it is the reason the floor was needed, and it is now pinned as evidence rather than left as an anecdote.
