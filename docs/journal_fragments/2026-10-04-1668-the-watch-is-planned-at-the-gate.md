The rule is event-driven on the content of the evidence, not a fixed poll and not a process check. A poll at interval T finds a failure T/2 late on average and spends a turn on every tick while the run is healthy; a watch that emits only on a stall past a stated bound, an error signature, process death or completion costs nothing while healthy and wakes at the failure. I did not measure either arm; that is arithmetic about the two designs. His 15 minutes is a sensible value for a stall bound on a phase that should heartbeat faster, which is a different thing from a poll interval. The older memory's worry, that a monitor emitting only on change never fires on a silent stall, is answered inside the rule: the stall is itself the event, defined on the progress marker's age, because a bare process match can be an orphaned watcher of a dead file. Lesson 1 sits under the visibility half: a poll ticking every 15 minutes is activity, and activity is not progress; the marker moving is progress, and it is also what the status file shows him.
