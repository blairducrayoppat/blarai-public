### 2026-08-07 — Three sessions called them someone else's files, and two of them were evidence

*Plain summary: Six stray files had been sitting in the project folder for weeks. Every session that noticed them assumed they belonged to another session and left them alone. Two of them turned out to be the input images for a performance test whose results we are still citing — meaning that test could not be re-run, and its recorded numbers could not be checked by anyone. The fix tracks the images and adds an automatic check so this cannot happen again quietly.*

The operator's instruction was blunt: *there is no one else, stop saying you won't touch someone else's work, you are responsible for everything.* He was right, and the proof was six files deep.

`git status` in the primary checkout had shown the same six untracked files for weeks. I had read past them all day. Two prior handoff cards had not: `class-census-landing-20260730.md:138` lists five of them, and `coder-tevv-landing-20260730b.md:36` lists four and notes the operator had offered to bin them. Both cards classified them the same way — another session's working set, flag but do not judge. That classification is the correct default in a shared tree, and it was wrong here, and nothing in the classification could tell you which.

So I opened them. `si.js` is a 317 KB Sphinx search index scraped from a third-party code-antipatterns documentation site, unreferenced, unattributed, sitting in a repository that syncs to a public mirror. `SoCWatchHelp.json` is an Intel tool's help output, regenerable. `blarai_logo_linkedin.jpg` is a brand asset that belongs with the others in `branding/`. Those three were clutter with a provenance question attached.

The other two were not clutter. `scripts/probe_35b_multimodal_quality.py` reads three images, and two of them — `blarai_lighthouse3.png` and `hand2.png` — were named as `_REPO_ROOT / "..."` and had never been committed. That script is gate B evidence for the 35B-A3B consolidation decision, which the status snapshot still carries as NOT DECIDABLE. Its results are in `docs/performance/`, dated 2026-07-16, and they cannot be re-derived by anyone, on any machine, including this one after a fresh clone.

That is a different failure from the one #1241 has been chasing all month. That arc is about an instrument that reports "did not run" as "ran and found nothing." This is *"ran, recorded, and not re-runnable"* — which at any later date is indistinguishable from fabricated. The evidence looked perfect. It had hardware, driver versions, methodology, verbatim outputs, the lot. It just could not be checked. And nothing in a 10,506-test gate noticed, for the simple reason that nothing checked whether a probe's *inputs* exist.

The fix that mattered was not committing two PNGs. It was `tests/security/test_measurement_subjects.py`: parse every tracked Python module, resolve every media path anchored to `Path(__file__)` through `.parent` chains and module-level constants built from them, and refuse any that names a file git does not track. Deny-by-default, because a blocklist of known-bad filenames would be exactly the shape I broke in a different lock the day before. A file the script *generates* is the only exception and must be declared with a reason; there are two, and I verified both by reading the scripts that write them rather than trusting the variable names.

Then the independent review took it apart, and it was worth every minute.

**It retracted one of my three claims.** I had said three subjects were unbanked. `cook_test.html` turned out to be byte-identical to `docs/performance/ov_cache_answer_flip_2026-07-22/subjects/cook_ok.html`, tracked since `56fd5782` on 2026-07-30 — the same blob, `8849e08c...`. My first-pass `md5sum` showed them as different files, and I took that as evidence of a missing original. They differed because one was CRLF in the working tree and one was LF, which is *the same line-ending fact the rest of my change is about*. I had the mechanism in hand and did not apply it to my own evidence. Two subjects were genuinely missing. The third claim was false, and false in the direction that made my change look more necessary — which is precisely the direction this whole arc exists to catch. It is recorded in the subjects README rather than quietly dropped, because a retraction that only lives in a commit message is not a retraction.

**It found the byte guard aimed at the wrong file.** I had marked `docs/performance/subjects/**` as `-text` so line-ending normalisation could not silently alter a subject, and wrote a confident comment about protecting ground truth. The actual ground truth for the gemma4 vision probe is `cook_ok.html`, in a *different* directory my glob did not reach, and it is being normalised right now: 23,713 bytes committed, 24,296 on disk. A re-render from the worktree would not have consumed the bytes the record cites. I had protected my copy and left the original exposed.

**It found the scanner blind to the repo's dominant idiom.** I handled `.parent` and not `parents[N]`, which is an `ast.Subscript` and appears in 20+ tracked modules; and `ast.Assign` but not `ast.AnnAssign`, which is the form our own strict-type-hints standard pushes authors toward. Neither made anything red. That is the whole danger: a blind spot costs coverage silently and reads as protection. Fixing both moved real coverage from 11 anchored references to 12, and the twelfth was `scripts/measure_guest_parser_memory.py`'s `news_quantum.html` — a subject of a recorded #661 measurement that my "structural control" could not see at all. It is now a canary in the guard test, so the scanner going blind again turns something red instead of quietly covering less.

The trade-off I took deliberately: the scanner is static and bounded. It cannot see f-strings, `os.path.join`, `joinpath`, a filename held in a variable, or a bare relative literal with no `/` join. The rejected alternative was to chase completeness with heuristics over the whole path-construction space, which would have produced false positives, which get a control disabled. Instead the module docstring *writes down every gap*, because an undocumented blind spot in a control reads as coverage, and that is worse than having no control at all. The reviewer ran 11 mutations against the production logic and found no test that stayed green when it should have gone red.

The uncomfortable part is the pattern, not the bug. I have spent this session insisting that a deferral needs an observable predicate and that a lock must be mutated before it is trusted — and I shipped a claim I had not checked against a blob, in a commit about claims outrunning evidence. The reviewer caught it in under ten minutes by running one command I never ran on my own artifact. Author is not verifier, and the day it feels most redundant is the day it pays.

Gate on merged `main`: 10515 passed / 0 failed / 0 skipped / 133 deselected, merged at `a2715515`. The +9 is this lock and its proofs.

**Proposed lesson:** *A recorded measurement is only evidence if its INPUTS are committed. Results, methodology and hardware in the record prove nothing if the subject the run consumed is untracked — the record is then indistinguishable from a fabricated one, and no amount of detail in it closes that gap. Check what a probe reads, not just what it wrote.*

**Proposed lesson:** *When you find the mechanism behind a bug, run it against your own evidence before you ship the finding. I discovered that CRLF normalisation makes identical files look different, and in the same change cited an md5 difference as proof that a file was missing.*
