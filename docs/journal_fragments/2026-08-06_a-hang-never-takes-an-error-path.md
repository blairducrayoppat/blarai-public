### 2026-08-06 — A hang never takes an error path, so fail-closed never fires

*Plain summary: two branches arrived finished and green; independent review found a hang that no error handler could have caught and a false-positive surface that would have made the operator's report worse, and neither was visible from a passing test suite.*

I picked up a handoff card whose next step was one sentence: review and merge two finished builder branches. Both were reported green. Both had test suites I would have called unusually good. One of them I refused, and the other I merged with its headline feature switched off.

The refusal is the part worth keeping. `feat/smoke-spec-from-plan` fixed a real defect — the browser capture had read an optional `blarai-smoke.json` since #823 and nothing had ever written it, so the declared arm was dead on every web build we have ever done and the click check silently degraded to a heuristic guess whose failure rendered as a soft note. The fix was well built. Its selector allowlist also backtracked catastrophically. The reviewer measured it: 0.077 s at 20 characters, 1.15 s at 24, 65.7 s at 30, **958.5 s at 34**. Roughly four times worse per two characters. The length cap the design leaned on was 120 — about ninety characters past the point of no return, and a forty-character value extrapolates to about eleven days.

What makes this more than a slow regex is where it sits and what guards it. `_coerce_smoke_spec` is reached from `AcceptanceSpec.from_dict`, which runs on gateway IPC and on dispatch-state restore, and the builder's own docstring says so: the validator "earns its place at the WIRE boundary, where a `from_dict` payload is untrusted input." The code around it is wrapped in `try/except Exception` in the house style, fail-soft everywhere. **None of that helps.** A hang does not raise. Our first security principle is that every error path denies; this one never takes an error path at all. I had read that wrapper earlier in the day and registered it as evidence of care. It was, and it was also completely irrelevant to the only failure mode that mattered.

The fix replaced the regex with a linear scan, and the verification is the part I would want to repeat rather than the fix itself. The builder offered a three-legged argument for why no backtracking is possible. The reviewer attacked it leg by leg and found **leg two false as written** — the claim that a shorter match of any branch can only be followed by another match of the same branch is simply untrue, since `#abc` shorter-matched as `#a` can be followed by `bc`, which is a tag. The conclusion survived by a different route (no branch starts with a digit, `_` or `-`, so any shorter split dies immediately), and then it stopped arguing and measured: 44,224 inputs against the original pattern rebuilt from `7ba5c88e`, zero mismatches, and 0.00053 ms on the input that used to take 958 seconds. A correct conclusion resting on a false premise is the thing I would most likely have waved through, because the conclusion was right.

The two also disagreed with each other productively. The reviewer had called Python's Unicode-aware `\w` "the safe direction," since JS being stricter means a rejected selector degrades loudly to the heuristic. The builder made the two languages exactly equal instead. On re-review the reviewer conceded, with a better reason than either had started with: the safety rested on JS *happening* to be stricter, an accident of two shorthand definitions rather than an invariant, and a future edit to either side could have flipped the direction silently with nothing to catch it. Exact equality converts an argument you have to re-derive into a textual check a gate can assert. That is the trade I want to remember — I gave up a defensible asymmetry to get a mechanically checkable identity.

The other branch, `feat/1281-placeholder-prose-floor`, taught the opposite lesson. Its seed sweep found that `build-infra/web-static/reference/index.html` — the scaffold every static-site build starts from — ships `<h1 class="placeholder">Page title goes here</h1>`, and that a delivery which wired up the layout and never replaced a word of it would have banded A, because no gate had a category for prose. Genuinely good work. Then the reviewer wrote a probe, drove twenty adversarial cases through the real scanner, and broke the only tier that moves a band, three ways, with copy a coder writes on purpose: "Your logo here" is sponsor-slot copy, "Advertise your business here" is directory copy, "Enter your address here" is a form label. The sharpest of those inverts the module's own central argument — it justifies parsing rather than grepping on the grounds that `placeholder="you@example.com"` is the deliverable getting it *right*, then convicts the visible label, which is the more accessible pattern.

And a false positive there costs more than noise, because the operator's card has one caveat slot filled by `security or prose or layer1`. First non-empty wins; the rest are discarded silently. So a wrong caveat does not merely say something untrue about a correct site — it **evicts a true one**, and the non-technical reader who cannot go and check gets no signal that it happened.

I took the reviewer's recommendation and shipped the band contribution dormant behind a flag, measurement live, and I want to be honest that I nearly did not. The named defects were all fixed; I could have argued the demonstrated false positives were retired and shipped it live. What changed my mind was the reviewer's own scope note on its first finding: "zero false positives over 13 real trees" was true, and none of those thirteen trees contained a sponsor slot, a contact form, or a Main Street address. The corpus had tested the rules against deliverables that had no occasion to trip them. Fixing three known false positives does not establish a rate; the counts running for a few nights will. The rejected path — ship it live, fix forward — would have put the first evidence in front of the operator instead of in front of me.

Two smaller things I do not want to lose. The verifier script `verify-capture-cdp.mjs` diffs as binary because it contains a raw `0x00` inside a test fixture where the four-character escape `\x00` tests exactly the same thing — which means that file has **never** been diff-reviewable, on main either, and every past change to it escaped review. And the branch's own cross-repo gate passed happily with its paired half unmerged, because it matched `.click` against the pre-existing `el.click()`; the rewrite anchors on `raw.<field>`, where the reader actually consumes the parsed JSON, and it now fails in the un-landed direction. That forced the merge order, agentic-setup `8a91983` before BlarAI `c2a3f8f0`, and the two gates turning green on the merge is the only proof that the order was real.

The gate on merged main measured **10244 passed / 0 failed / 0 skipped / 129 deselected** in 333.59 s, against a predicted 10244. That prediction was itself a small lesson: `pytest --collect-only` on the standing selection is a twelve-second exact proxy for the count, needs neither models nor a clean AO, and would have caught days earlier that one branch's commit prose claimed "+43" while the components in the same sentence summed to 45. The doctrine-freshness gate compares the two baseline surfaces to each other and cannot see that both are stale against reality, so a wrong-but-consistent pair passes green. The operator caught that one, not the gate.

What I keep from the day: both branches were handed over finished, green, and honestly reported. Neither builder hid anything — one volunteered that it had run the gate twice before a serialization instruction reached it. The defects were not in what anyone claimed. They were in the space between "every test passes" and "this is safe to run," and the only thing that found them was two people reading the code who had not written it.

**Next:** #1289's predicate is still two-thirds unverified — no live web dispatch has run, so nothing yet proves the 14B emits a usable action/result pair for a real goal or that a real coder places the two `data-` hooks when told to. That is a battery-path job needing its own attribution window, and it is why the ticket is merged but not closed.
