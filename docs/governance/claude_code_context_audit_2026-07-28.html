How sessions should end: an audit of the handoff practice

Research read for the Lead Architect · 2026-07-28 · Sources: the official Claude Code documentation (fetched live today) plus twelve full-read practitioner sources from 2025–2026, gathered by two research agents · Local evidence measured in this repository. Nothing in current practice has been changed — every proposed change below waits for your decision. Revised same day at your direction: the redesign section now carries the execution-ready build plan (A1–A8) for capturing facts in their durable homes at event time.

Verdict on your question. Handing off to a fresh session is not poor practice — it is the approach Anthropic itself endorses and the one experienced users converge on. What the evidence does condemn is how we do it: our handoff documents are roughly ten times larger than the field's standard, they are written at the moment of maximum fatigue rather than at healthy boundaries, and they buffer information that should have been filed in its permanent home hours earlier.

The deep change you suspected is real, but it points at the handoff layer, not the foundation. The foundation — durable state on disk (the ticket board, the journal, the lessons file, memory) plus fresh sessions that re-ground from disk — is precisely the strongest published pattern. The redesign below (R1–R7) shrinks briefs by ~80%, replaces the triggers, retires one stop-rule the tool has outgrown, and puts two governance questions squarely in front of you.

1 · What was asked

Two questions: (a) what is the best method for a Claude Code developer whose session has reached full context and wants to continue in a fresh session, and (b) is handing off itself a poor practice — should something else entirely be used? Per your instruction, current practice was treated as suspect, not as a baseline to defend.

2 · What the tool actually does today (verified against live docs, 2026-07-28)

First, the vocabulary. The context window is a session's working memory — roughly 200,000 tokens, on the order of 150,000 words. Everything the session reads, writes, and runs accumulates there. When it nears full, Claude Code performs compaction: it replaces the conversation with a summary and continues. That summary is lossy — some detail is genuinely gone.

Facts that matter for our practice (each verified in the official documentation today):
  • Compaction is no longer a cliff. When it fires, the project's CLAUDE.md and the first 200 lines of the memory index are automatically re-injected from disk, a preservation order favors the task and key code over old tool output, and the running harness explicitly instructs sessions to keep working through the boundary rather than wrap up early. Runaway loops are detected and stopped after ~3 cycles.
  • What compaction loses: nested per-directory instruction files, the skills index, and any conversational nuance the summary didn't keep. A standing instruction in CLAUDE.md can tell the summarizer what to always preserve — we don't use this yet.
  • Manual controls: /context shows how full the window is; /compact accepts steering instructions ("keep the open defects and modified files"); /clear starts fresh (the old conversation is now saved and resumable); /memory shows what is permanently remembered.
  • Session continuity exists natively: claude --resume reopens a previous session with its memory intact (for interruptions, not exhaustion); /branch forks one. There is no built-in handoff feature — handoff documents are a user-space pattern, which is why quality varies so widely in the field.
  • Subagents are the context shield: each runs in its own separate window and returns only a short summary (~200–400 tokens) to the main session. Their file reading never lands in the main window. This is the official mechanism for long-running work.
  • Always-loaded files have a recommended budget: the docs advise keeping each memory file to ~200 lines for adherence, with conditional content moved to path-scoped rule files (.claude/rules/) that load only when relevant files are touched, and @import for on-demand documents.

3 · What the field converged on

Across the official best-practices guidance, Anthropic's context-engineering essay, and the practitioner literature, the same four-layer model appears again and again. It is a ladder — the higher layers make the lower ones cheap.

LayerPractice
1 · PreventOne task per session; start a new session for a new task ("the kitchen-sink session" is the officially named #1 failure pattern). Delegate bulk reading and research to subagents. Keep always-loaded instruction files lean. After two failed corrections of the same defect, stop pushing — a fresh session with a better-informed prompt "almost always outperforms a long session with accumulated corrections" (official guidance).
2 · Externalize continuouslyDurable state lives on disk as it is created — plans, decisions, progress, and failures go to files, trackers, and commit history at event time, not at session end. Anthropic's essay names this "structured note-taking"; the strongest autonomous pattern in the wild (the "Ralph loop") runs fresh contexts in a circle over a perpetually-current plan file and treats any per-session handoff narrative as a symptom of state that failed to reach its home.
3 · Compact deliberatelyMid-task, compact at a natural boundary with explicit instructions, never by letting the automatic version fire mid-thought. A large practitioner camp disables auto-compaction entirely for state-heavy work; a 2026 bug that silently stripped resumed sessions of their reasoning hardened that distrust. The 1-million-token windows now available change none of this — attention degrades as windows fill ("context rot"), so the ceiling rose but the discipline stands.
4 · Hand off cleanlyAt a genuine boundary, write a short continuation document — goal, status, decisions with reasoning, what failed and why, exact next step, test baseline — then start fresh and have the successor read it and verify against the repository. Write it at ~60% context use while still sharp; "a degraded author writes a degraded handoff" is the field's most-cited failure. The counter-finding: oversized handoffs actively harm the successor ("context-dump fallacy" — noise degrades reasoning). Field-standard size: tens of lines.

The ~60% number is converged folklore (several independent practitioners, no controlled measurement) — treat it as a planning heuristic, not physics.

4 · The audit: our practice against that standard

Measured locally today: 137 handoff briefs, ~2.1 MB over the project's life — roughly half a million tokens of continuity prose. The last 8 days alone: 17 files, ~456 KB; median brief ~22 KB (~5,500 tokens); the largest 87 KB. Each transition costs the successor its reading (~5–6k tokens) plus re-grounding plus a full nine-point comprehension gate, before any work begins — and costs you a gate confirmation 2–4 times per day. Meanwhile every session (and most subagents) starts by loading 317 lines / ~48 KB of CLAUDE.md plus the 18.6 KB memory index: ~8% of the window spent before the first action.

5 · The redesign — proposed, nothing applied

Each item stands alone; ratify or decline per item. "MINE" = a technical change I implement on your go-ahead. "YOURS" = a governance trade-off only you should weigh.

R1 · Bank state when it happens; shrink the brief to a landing card [MINE — technical]

Rule: any fact that matters tomorrow reaches its durable home the moment it exists — the ticket gets its comment when the thing happens, the journal fragment is written at ship time, the unblocked ticket is annotated in the same motion as the merge that unblocked it. The brief then carries only what has no durable home yet: machine state (processes, branch, tree), in-flight thread position, retractions and dead ends not yet banked, the single next step, and the anchors block. Target ≤150 lines; the existing verifier gains a size check so this is enforced, not aspirational. The standing operations block moves to one tracked document that every brief cites by path. Trade-off: slightly more bookkeeping during the session; in exchange, transitions stop costing three documents' worth of reading, and the 2026-07-22 class of omission (the brief forgot what the board knew) becomes structurally impossible because the board was updated at event time.

Execution-ready: the full build plan is specified below — “R1 in full” (A1–A8).

R2 · Hand off at boundaries, planned by the gauge [YOURS — changes when you get interrupted]

Retire the degradation-shaped triggers (post-compaction, quality slipping, hour counts). New rule: sessions check /context at natural seams; from ~60% usage they plan the landing (finish the current limb, close its ticket, write the thin brief), landing by ~75–80% while still sharp. A handoff authored after degradation is the field's canonical failure and currently our default timing. Trade-off: sessions end slightly earlier than their theoretical maximum; you get briefs written by a sharp author instead of an exhausted one.

R3 · Compaction becomes a speed bump, not a stop sign [YOURS — retires a stop-rule]

Current doctrine treats a compaction as a signal to consider stopping. The tool has outgrown this: doctrine and memory re-inject automatically, and the harness is built to continue through the boundary. New rule: mid-arc compaction → re-read the live surfaces (ticket, branch, plan), confirm position, continue; the full handoff ceremony is reserved for genuine session ends. We also add standing compaction instructions to CLAUDE.md ("always preserve: current ticket, branch, gate status, modified files, open defects") — a documented feature we don't use. Trade-off: a summarized session is trusted to keep working mid-arc; the mitigation is R1 (state is already banked, so the summary carries navigation, not cargo). For judgment-heavy moments a session may still choose the boundary — the change is the default, not a prohibition.

R4 · Slim the always-loaded doctrine — staged, carefully [YOURS — CLAUDE.md is governance]

CLAUDE.md is 317 lines / ~48 KB against a ~200-line official adherence guidance; every session and most subagents pay it before working, and the docs warn that bloated instruction files get partially ignored. Proposal: keep a lean always-on core (critical rules, decision boundary, identity split, pointers), move conditional content to path-scoped rules that load only when relevant surfaces are touched, and on-demand documents via imports. The freshness gates that police doctrine move with their content. This is the riskiest item — the file demonstrably works, adherence loss here is unmeasured locally, and a bad split could orphan rules. Staged migration with the doctrine-freshness tests extended first, or decline and accept the load as the price of a single authoritative file. My recommendation: do it, slowly, one section at a time.

R5 · Tier the gate [YOURS — entirely]

Today every successor session authors the full nine-point comprehension gate and waits for you — 2–4 times a day at the current handoff rate. Option A (recommended): full gate for new engagements; for a continuation of already-gated work, a delta-gate of ≤10 lines — what changed, what I'll do next, what I need from you — still confirmed by you, but readable in twenty seconds. Option B: keep the full gate everywhere and accept the cost knowingly; it is your strongest comprehension check, and R1/R6 will reduce how often it fires anyway. The trade-off is your attention against depth-of-verification on continuations; only you can price your attention.

R6 · Prefer arcs to marathons [YOURS — touches the autonomy doctrine]

The "driver" sessions that run the whole queue until context exhausts are our version of the officially named kitchen-sink anti-pattern, and they are why briefs must hand over a world. Proposal: a session takes one arc to done (merged, ticket closed, journal written — which is when there is almost nothing to hand off), then either picks the next arc if the gauge is healthy or lands. Queue-driving continuity comes from the board, not from any one session's longevity. Trade-off: more session starts (each paying the always-loaded cost — which R4 shrinks); in exchange, each session works mostly in its sharp zone and handoffs become boring.

R7 · Carry the dead ends; adopt the two-corrections rule [MINE — technical]

The brief template gains a required "What failed" section — approaches tried and abandoned, with why — distinct from retractions (claims corrected). Field consensus calls this the most load-bearing content a successor receives; without it they cheerfully retry the dead end. And doctrine adopts the official two-corrections rule: when two attempted fixes for the same defect have failed, stop grinding — bank what was learned and put fresh context on it (subagent or fresh session). This matches your existing stop-doomed-runs instinct; it just names the threshold.

R1 in full — the build plan for banking at event time (A1–A8, execution-ready)

Your instruction: be structurally set up to capture facts in their durable homes automatically and systematically, at event time, so a handoff hands over objectives, deltas, and pointers — not a rebuilt world. The principle becomes structure in three moves: doctrine names every fact's home and its write moment (A1, A6, A7); the brief becomes physically too small to be a world (A2, A3); tooling automates the volatile half and machine-checks the rest (A4, A5, A8). Vigilance is not one of the mechanisms — every rule below has an enforcement surface, because a rule without a control is a hope.

The governing habit is the crash test, run at every seam of the work: if this session vanished right now, with no brief written, what would be lost? Anything you can name is a banking failure — and the fix is to bank it at that moment, not to remember it for the handoff.

Every fact lands in its durable home AT THE EVENT, never at handoff:
- Work state changes (limb done, blocker hit, scope shift) → dated CURRENT STATE comment on the ticket, at the change.
- Decision made → the causing ticket at decision time; governance decisions → ADR + DECISION_REGISTER row in the same change.
- Approach abandoned → "DEAD END: tried X, failed because Y" ticket comment at abandonment; transferable judgment → LESSONS, mechanics → FIELD_NOTES.

- Merge lands → the SAME atomic motion comments every ticket the merge UNBLOCKS (sweep blocked-by predicates at merge time — the moment of maximum knowledge).
- Ship narrative → journal fragment at ship time. Measurement → PERFORMANCE_LOG + docs/performance at run time. (Standing rules, restated here as the banking table's rows.)

Writing one takes minutes and teaches you nothing — if it takes longer, or you learn something while writing it, banking failed upstream; fix that, not the brief.

6 · Direct answers

(a) Best method when a session reaches full context: ideally, don't reach it — land at a boundary around 60–75% with the arc closed and a thin, machine-verified landing card (R1/R2). If mid-arc, let compaction carry you through, re-verify against the live surfaces, and land at the next seam (R3). Reserve --resume for interruptions of healthy sessions; never paste a summary into a new prompt — point the fresh session at disk and let it verify.

(b) Is handing off poor practice? No — it is the endorsed, converged practice, and your instinct to externalize state and re-ground fresh sessions from disk is the right architecture. What the evidence says to abandon is the current dosage: mega-briefs, degradation-timed authorship, per-brief rebuilt world-state, and treating every compaction as an emergency. The "something else entirely" you asked about does exist, and you already own it: the durable substrate (board, journal, lessons, memory). The deep change is moving weight off the briefs and onto that substrate, at event time.

7 · Your side of the keyboard — a short cheat sheet

  • New job → new session. Don't extend an old chat into unrelated work; it drags noise forward.
  • A session failed the same fix twice? Say: "bank what you learned and hand off to fresh context." Fresh + informed beats long + frustrated.
  • Interrupted (reboot, closed lid)? claude --resume reopens the session intact — no handoff needed.
  • Curious how full a session is? Type /context. Green is roomy; plan landings from ~60%.
  • See a compaction notice mid-task? Under the new rules that's normal — expect the session to re-check its ticket and continue, not stop.
  • Memory: /memory shows what persists across all sessions. Our index is at 123 of the 200 auto-loaded lines — headroom, but worth watching.
